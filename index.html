My First Minecraft Experience

August 23, 2026 by Brandon Allen

When i first got into minecraft i had the help of a friend who helped me with the set up. So when i opened the game for the first time the game looked completely different from what i had seen online. My friend had installed shaders to make the game look more beautiful. I spent the first few minutes of the game just walking around admiring the scenery. It was a beautiful sight with the sun's rays reflecting off the water and the trees swaying in the wind. I had no idea what i was supposed to be doing, so i just kept walking and taking it all in. As i was wandering aimlessly the soundtrack kicked in and it made the experience so much more immersive. I genuinely forgot this was a survival game.

Before i knew it though, it was night time and i had only managed to make the basic wooden tools. I would come to regret my decision of being so lackadaisical. Though still nice to the eyes, the monsters had started to spawn in, and it went from a calm experience to screaming and shouting for help while my friend laughs so hard he falls off his chair [ He has been here the entire time :| ]. As the night went on i ran from zombies, skeletons, spiders and creepers until i could eventually find refuge in a small hole in the wall where i blocked myself off, locked in complete darkness, just waiting for the sun to rise. This was not the best way to start of a game. I had no torches, no bed, no plan. Just regret and a sense of relief that i got away from the enemies beyond the dirt wall. When morning finally came i burst out of my dirt hole like i had just survived a war, i stared at the sun for solid 30 seconds relieved that i survived and made a mental note to not let this happen again. I then began to curse out my friend after realizing he left me for dead (in game) with no indication that this would happen. In response i will be forcing him to watch saltburn as repentance for his actions, i will not suffer alone. Anyways, a 10/10 experience, would recommend.

Building a Japanese Shrine in Survival

October 6, 2026 by Brandon Allen

I've been playing the game consistently for a while now, and after finally getting my own base set up and getting settled in, i decided to build something special at a biome i had discovered about 2 weeks into playing. It was a gorgeous mix of a spruce biome and a cherry blossom forest, surrounded by a small lake with mountains wrapping around the whole area. The pink petals from the cherry trees would float down onto the water surface due to the effect of the shaders and the spruce trees towered over everything like a natural cathedral. It was almost too perfect to not build something there, so i decided to make a Japanese shrine with a giant torii gate as the entrance. I spent a good hour just standing in the biome figuring out the best angle and positioning for the build before placing a single block.

The most challenging part wasn't designing or actually building the structures, it was gathering the materials. The wood for the gate was made from crimson stem trees which are only found in the Nether, so besides having to venture into a place that is 90% lava and 10% hope and prayers, i also had to search for a crimson forest biome, gather enough logs and somehow survive the whole trip. Long story short, i died a lot but i got it done. The path leading up to the shrine was lit using sea lanterns and regular lanterns placed under the water, with the walkway itself made from a mix of gravel, dirt, deepslate, netherrack and cobblestone for a weathered, ancient feel. The path leads up to a wooden bridge which then opens into the main shrine building, constructed from the same crimson stem logs and spruce wood as the gate. The finished result honestly exceeded my expectations and is probably my favourite build i've made so far.
